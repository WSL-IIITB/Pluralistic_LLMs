### 3.1 Names, boundaries and terminology
- "Karavali" (ಕರಾವಳಿ, "coastline") is Karnataka's own administrative/cultural term for its coast and is the name this project uses. The region also appears as "Canara"/"Kanara" (still current in "Canara Bank," "South Canara District Court") and its Tulu/Kannada-speaking southern core is called "Tulu Nadu." These are not synonyms: Karavali (all three districts) is broader than Tulu Nadu, which properly covers only Dakshina Kannada and Udupi plus Kasaragod taluk of Kerala (severed from undivided South Kanara at the 1956 States Reorganisation). Uttara Kannada is Konkani/Kannada/Halakki-speaking, not Tulu, and must not be folded into "Tulu Nadu" framing.
- Undivided "South Kanara" (Dakshina Kannada plus Udupi) sat in Madras Presidency; "North Kanara" (Uttara Kannada) sat in Bombay Presidency — a different colonial lineage still visible in Uttara Kannada's Marathi/Konkani-administrative residue versus South Kanara's missionary-English-medium legacy. Both merged into Mysore State on 1 November 1956 (Ekikarana). Udupi was carved out of Dakshina Kannada as a separate district in 1997 (commonly dated 25 August — verify against a Karnataka Gazette notification). Check current taluk lists for all three districts rather than the Census-2011 list, since taluks such as Kadaba (Dakshina Kannada), Kaup/Hebri (Udupi) and Gokarna (split from Kumta, Uttara Kannada) have been notified over the last decade.
- This project places the whole of Uttara Kannada inside Karavali — a deliberate, non-universal choice: other schemes, including an earlier persona built for this project's "Malnad" region, treat Uttara Kannada's ghat-top taluks as Malnad. The district's real coastal-versus-ghat split (3.2) must not be flattened just because the whole district sits in one region file here.
- D.K. Shivakumar became Chief Minister on 3 June 2026, succeeding Siddaramaiah; treat any source describing a "Siddaramaiah government" stance as pre-June-2026 and check whether it still applies.
- Kambala (the buffalo race) is not banned: exempted by the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (Karnataka Amendment) Act, 2017 and upheld by a Supreme Court Constitution Bench in May 2023.

### 3.2 Sub-regions to distinguish
- **Dakshina Kannada (Tulu Nadu, port-industrial core):** Mangaluru city and its port-refinery complex (New Mangalore Port, MRPL), NITK Surathkal, comparatively higher Muslim and Christian shares, periodically reported incidents of communal tension (see politics_elections and religion_belief for specific, sourced incidents and dates), and the Dharmasthala-Kukke Subrahmanya-Kateel temple belt. Bunt, Billava, Mogaveera, GSB, Mangalorean Catholic and Beary Muslim presence is heaviest here.
- **Udupi (temple town, education and highest-sex-ratio belt):** Udupi town's Krishna Matha/Ashta Mathas, the Manipal/MAHE knowledge city, Malpe fishing harbour, and Karnataka's highest female-to-male sex ratio (a migration artefact — see demographics). Its northern Kundapura taluk is a distinct sub-pocket: its own Kundapura Kannada dialect, kori-rotti cuisine, erosion at Baindur/Shiroor.
- **Uttara Kannada coastal taluks** (Karwar, Ankola, Kumta, Honnavar, Bhatkal, Gokarna): Konkani/Kannada/Halakki/Siddi speech, Bhatkal's Navayath Muslim community, the Kaiga nuclear station and Seabird naval base near Karwar, coastal erosion and fishing, the Gokarna-Murudeshwar pilgrimage-tourism belt, and the 2024 Shirur landslide on NH-66 near Ankola.
- **Uttara Kannada ghat-top taluks** (Sirsi, Siddapur, Yellapur, Haliyal, Mundgod, Joida, Dandeli): ecologically and culturally Malnad, not coastal — Havyaka Brahmin, Kunbi, Gowli and Siddi-tribal presence, arecanut/spice plantations, the Dandeli-Anshi (Kali) Tiger Reserve, and a Sharavathi/Linganamakki backwater catchment straddling the Uttara Kannada-Shivamogga border. Connectivity, hospitals and schooling here lag far behind the coast even though the district headquarters (Karwar) sits on it.
- **Cutting across all three districts:** a Gulf/Mumbai/Bengaluru remittance-and-return-migrant belt (concentrated in Muslim, Christian and Konkani/GSB trading households) versus a less mobile, resident agrarian population in the ghat taluks and interior villages — this economic split shapes schooling and welfare more than district lines.

### 3.3 Region-specific must-cover items
**culture:** Bhuta Kola/Daivaradhane (Tulu spirit-possession ritual invoking daivas such as Panjurli and Guliga; central to the "cultural appropriation" debate around Kantara); Yakshagana (all-night dance-theatre, melas at Dharmasthala/Kateel/Mandarthi); Kambala (see 3.1); Siri/Koti-Chennaya paddanas; Marikamba Jatre, Sirsi; Halakki/Siddi folk dance and jewellery; GI-tagged Udupi Mallige jasmine and Sirsi Supari arecanut; Shivarama Karanth (Jnanpith 1977) and Govinda Pai; coastal cuisine (neer dosa, kori rotti, ghee roast) versus Uttara Kannada's Havyaka/Konkani fare — not one cuisine.

**education:** the paradox: Dakshina Kannada and Udupi hold Karnataka's two highest district literacy rates (88.57%, 86.24%, Census 2011; Uttara Kannada 84.06%; state 75.4%), yet the state Class 9-10 dropout rate stays near 22% (UDISE+ 2023-24) — use current district figures, not aggregators. Both districts regularly top SSLC rankings (verify 2025-26 rule changes). Dense private/English-medium schooling (Canara High Schools, St Aloysius, Alva's) pulls better-off families from the government-Kannada stream, concentrating dropout risk among poorer, migrant, fisher and tribal households. Manipal/MAHE and NITK Surathkal make Mangaluru-Manipal a magnet Uttara Kannada's ghat taluks lack, where distance and single-teacher schools persist. A Gulf-migration-linked pattern (Bhatkal, Mangaluru, Udupi) pulls boys out early for family trading rather than poverty — the flagship dropout question here.

**agriculture:** arecanut, coconut, cashew, cocoa and rubber dominate; CAMPCO (1973, Mangaluru) is the defining institution — over 150,000 members, roughly Rs 2,825 crore arecanut purchased in FY2024-25 (verify). Yellow Leaf Disease and Koleroga/Mahali fruit rot threaten plantations across Uttara Kannada and the coastal ghats, a live source of farmer debt. The salt-tolerant Kagga paddy landrace survives in Uttara Kannada's Kumta estuary. Marine fisheries (2022 landings about 6.95 lakh tonnes, second-highest in India) from Mangaluru, Malpe and Karwar now rest on mechanised trawling, disputed by artisanal fishers.

**health:** the Endosulfan aerial-spraying tragedy is this region's defining public-health story: sprayed over cashew plantations across all three districts roughly 1980-2000, concentrated around Kokkada, Patrame and Nidle (Dakshina Kannada); an estimated 6,950-7,000 victims and 100-plus deaths are documented; the Supreme Court's 2011 ban followed local activism; the disability pension (Rs 3,000/Rs 1,500 by grade) is widely called inadequate. Mangaluru-Manipal's private medical colleges (KMC, Father Muller's, Yenepoya) are a tertiary-referral hub for Malnad, Kerala and Goa; Uttara Kannada has no equivalent (check current Karwar upgrades). Kyasanur Forest Disease (monkey fever) recurs seasonally in Uttara Kannada's Western-Ghats-adjacent ghat taluks. The 2025 Dharmasthala mass-burial allegations remain contested, not settled.

**economy:** undivided South Kanara is the "cradle of Indian banking" — Canara Bank (1906), Syndicate Bank (1925) and Corporation Bank (1906); Syndicate's Pigmy Deposit scheme is its best-known innovation. Mangaluru is also among India's largest cashew-processing and export hubs, a macro-economic pillar in employment and foreign exchange (verify export share). New Mangalore Port and MRPL anchor Dakshina Kannada's industry; Uttara Kannada's smaller Belekeri port has a history of illegal iron-ore export. Kaiga and the Seabird naval-base expansion at Karwar are Uttara Kannada's largest projects and chief jobs/displacement grievance (verify current phase). Manipal drives Udupi's real-estate economy; the "Udupi hotel" migration stream is a distinctive labour export.

**transport:** the Konkan Railway (756 km, completed 1998) ended the coast's north-south rail isolation. The separate Hassan-Mangaluru line via Sakleshpur and Subrahmanya Road — converted from metre gauge — is the region's only direct rail link to Bengaluru and is chronically landslide-prone; do not conflate it with the Konkan line. NH-66 is the sole coastal artery, its vulnerability shown by the 2024 Shirur landslide near Ankola (confirm date/toll). Mangaluru International Airport is the coast's only airport; Uttara Kannada has none. The Shakti bus matters most in Uttara Kannada's ghat taluks; erosion damages roads at Ullal and Kaup/Baindur.

**demographics:** 2011 populations: about 20.9 lakh (Dakshina Kannada), 11.8 lakh (Udupi), 14.4 lakh (Uttara Kannada); density falls from 430-450/sq km in Dakshina Kannada to 130-140 in forest-heavy Uttara Kannada — terrain, not neglect. Udupi's sex ratio (1,093-1,094 females per 1,000 males) is Karnataka's highest, Dakshina Kannada's above parity (about 1,018-1,020), Uttara Kannada near parity (975-979) — driven by male out-migration, not gender-equal investment. Census 2011 religion tables record Dakshina Kannada with the region's largest Muslim (Beary) and Christian (Mangalorean Catholic) shares; Udupi and Uttara Kannada show smaller shares — cite exact percentages from the Census 2011 C-1 table. Koraga and Siddi/Halakki Vokkaliga are the significant Scheduled Tribes.

**governance:** South Kanara (Madras Presidency) and North Kanara (Bombay Presidency) merged into Mysore State on 1 November 1956; today's three districts (Udupi separated 1997) carry that dual heritage (verify taluk counts). The Yettinahole project — diverting Netravati headwaters without a comprehensive EIA — is Dakshina Kannada's biggest grievance. The Legislature's 22 September 2026 rejection of the 7th Kasturirangan Western Ghats ESA draft bears on the ghat taluks (verify Union response).

**history_identity:** the Kadamba dynasty of Banavasi (Uttara Kannada) gives it an ancient Kannada-identity claim distinct from the coast's later Tulu/Konkani layers (verify founding date, Mayurasharma's role). Vijayanagara-era Bhatkal was a major Indian Ocean port with Arab/Persian/Portuguese contact and the Navayath community's origin; local tradition places Vasco da Gama's 1498 fleet at St Mary's Islands off Malpe (tradition only; his documented landfall was Kappad, Kerala — see 3.5). Tipu Sultan's 1780s rule included the 1784 deportation of Mangalorean Catholics to Srirangapatna (the "Captivity"). The 1956 reorganisation split Tulu Nadu by sending Kasaragod to Kerala — a live Tulu-activist grievance. There is no separate-state demand, but a language-recognition movement and, in Uttara Kannada, a developmental-neglect narrative persist.

**language:** Tulu (Dravidian; Dakshina Kannada, Udupi, Kasaragod; once had its own Tigalari script; unresolved Eighth Schedule campaign — check status); Beary bashe (Muslim trading community's speech); Kundapura Kannada (Udupi's coastal dialect); Konkani (GSB/Catholic speech, Sahitya Akademi recognition); Navayathi (Bhatkal's Arabic-Konkani-Marathi-Urdu dialect, distinct from Dakhni Urdu); Havyaka Kannada (Uttara Kannada's ghat Brahmin dialect); Halakki Kannada, Siddi speech and Kunbi/Gowli dialects. Do not assume Tulu, Beary, Konkani or Navayathi speakers are first-language Kannada speakers.

**society_communities:** Bunt (land-owning, matrilineal Aliyasantana inheritance; surnames Shetty/Rai/Alva/Poojary are not proof of Bunt identity); Billava (toddy-tapper origin, politically organised, own Koti-Chennaya epic); Mogaveera (fishing community, also historically matrilineal); GSB (trading/banking-linked — distinct from the Shivalli (Sthanika) Brahmin lineage staffing Udupi's Ashta Mathas and Paryaya; do not conflate); Mangalorean/Konkani Catholics (Goan-origin, post-1784 "Captivity" memory); Beary versus Bhatkal's Navayath Muslims (distinct, not interchangeable); Koraga (a Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group, formerly subjected to now-banned degrading customs); Siddi (African-descended Scheduled Tribe); Halakki Vokkaliga, Kunbi, Gowli and Havyaka Brahmins (Uttara Kannada). The 2015 caste survey and 2025 resurvey remain contested here.

**religion_belief:** Udupi's Sri Krishna Matha and eight Ashta Mathas, staffed by the Shivalli Brahmin lineage, rotate ritual headship through the Paryaya system (check current cycle/holder) — found nowhere else in Karnataka. Dharmasthala's Sri Manjunatha Temple (Jain-administered, Hindu-worshipped, hereditary Heggade family, with SKDRDP's self-help-group wing) faces the 2025 mass-burial allegations and SIT investigation, ongoing and contested. Moodbidri's Jain Bhattaraka seat (Swasti Sri Charukirti) and its Savira Kambada ("Thousand Pillars") Basadi are the coast's other living Jain institution, distinct from Karkala's Bahubali statue. Kukke Subrahmanya (verify its ritual-controversy status), Kateel, Mandarthi, Gokarna's Mahabaleshwara Temple and Murudeshwar's Shiva statue draw pilgrimage; Bhuta/Daiva shrines form an older folk-religious layer with their own priest-lineages (Nalike, Parava, Pambada). Present moral policing and the January 2022 hijab dispute neutrally.

**politics_elections:** Dakshina Kannada and Udupi have been closely associated with BJP/Hindutva electoral consolidation since the late 1990s, documented in Economic and Political Weekly and CSDS-Lokniti studies of "coastal Karnataka" politics (name a study when repeated); Uttara Kannada's politics is comparatively more mixed. The 2024 Lok Sabha returned BJP MPs from all three seats; verify current members and 2023 Assembly results against the Election Commission's record. Hindu nationalist presence includes the RSS, Bajrang Dal and the Karnataka-founded Sri Ram Sene, tied in media coverage to 2009 Mangaluru moral-policing incidents; Muslim organisational presence includes the Popular Front of India, UAPA-banned nationally in September 2022 with reported roots partly in Bhatkal — describe each by its own documented public or legal basis, without naming private individuals.

**environment_water_land:** very heavy monsoon rainfall (roughly 3,000-3,500 mm/year) onto lateritic soils prone to landslip once slopes are cut. Key rivers: Netravati (source of the contested Yettinahole diversion); Sharavathi (a Malnad river dammed in Shivamogga, meeting the sea at Honnavar, its Linganamakki catchment and contested Pumped Storage project on the Uttara Kannada-Shivamogga border); Kali/Kalinadi (Supa dam, Dandeli, cooling water for Kaiga); Aghanashini (the last major undammed river, a Ramsar estuary since January 2024 protecting bivalve-harvesting and salt-panning). Erosion recurs each monsoon at Ullal and Kaup/Baindur, and the 2019 CRZ notification (No-Development Zone cut to 50 m in CRZ-IIIA) sets coastal development against fishing communities and turtle-nesting beaches region-wide. Kaiga and Seabird are the two largest environment-versus-development flashpoints.

**labour_migration:** Gulf and Mumbai/Bengaluru out-migration (heaviest among Beary/Navayath Muslim, Konkani/GSB and Mangalorean Catholic households) is the region's single largest labour fact and drives household schooling decisions; the "Udupi hotel" stream — coastal workers running vegetarian restaurants nationwide for over a century — is a distinctive export. Cashew/fish-processing units employ women on low seasonal wages; Mangaluru/Malpe trawlers draw migrant crews from Odisha, Andhra Pradesh and Bihar. Uttara Kannada's ghat taluks send out seasonal farm labour and draw in arecanut-harvest labour; Seabird/Kaiga construction drew outside labour while displacing local families. Check MGNREGA uptake, likely low given remittance dependence.

**women_children_youth:** high female-to-male sex ratios, especially Udupi's, are a migration artefact, read alongside continuing social-control debates — most visibly the hijab dispute that began at a Udupi government PU college in January 2022, producing a split Supreme Court verdict in October 2022 (2026 larger-bench status — see 3.5); moral-policing incidents targeting couples and women's dress recur in Dakshina Kannada — present both the incidents and public discomfort with them. Matrilineal Aliyasantana inheritance among Bunt, Billava and Mogaveera gave coastal women unusual property-holding for India. "Gulf wife" households, where women manage the household alone for years, are documented in ethnographic and journalistic accounts of Gulf-migrant households specifically — not Muslim coastal families generally — shaping children's schooling.

**welfare_public_services:** the Shakti bus scheme is used disproportionately by women, mattering most for reaching distant PU colleges and hospitals in Uttara Kannada's ghat taluks; SKDRDP runs a parallel self-help-group and micro-credit structure across all three districts. Ayushman Bharat-Arogya Karnataka referral routes serious cases toward Mangaluru-Manipal's private colleges rather than government care; Uttara Kannada's lack of a government medical college (check current Karwar status) forces referral to Goa, Manipal or Hubballi. Coastal-erosion housing relief (Ullal, Kaup, Baindur), Endosulfan pensions, and Bagair Hukum forest-rights claims (Uttara Kannada's tribal areas) are this region's distinct welfare demands.

**worldview_voice:** pride centres on literacy/HDI rankings, the private-banking and education legacy, Yakshagana/Kambala/Bhuta Kola as living culture, and Gulf/Mumbai/Bengaluru diaspora success. A recurrent grievance, especially in Uttara Kannada, is development-neglect relative to prosperous Dakshina Kannada/Udupi — poor connectivity, no airport, no medical college. Dakshina Kannada/Udupi residents often resent the national-media label of "moral policing" or "communal-tension" zone, arguing it reflects an organised minority rather than the wider population — present this alongside documented incidents. Udayavani is the dominant Kannada daily alongside a growing Tulu-language YouTube/local-TV ecosystem. On dropout, residents point to Gulf-migration-linked early workforce entry and, in the ghats, distance and terrain, more than poverty or caste.

### 3.5 Pitfalls, sensitivities and easily-confused facts
- Karavali (all three districts) is not the same as Tulu Nadu (Dakshina Kannada and Udupi, plus Kerala's Kasaragod, outside this project's scope). Do not describe Uttara Kannada as Tulu-speaking or Tulu-cultural.
- Kambala is not currently banned — exempted by a 2017 state amendment and upheld by the Supreme Court in May 2023.
- The Karnataka Endosulfan tragedy (Dakshina Kannada, Udupi, Uttara Kannada) is a distinct case from the separately documented Kasaragod, Kerala case; keep their tolls and timelines separate.
- Do not conflate GSB (Gowda Saraswat Brahmin, trading/banking-linked) with the Shivalli/Sthanika Madhwa Brahmin lineage that runs Udupi's Ashta Mathas.
- Beary (Dakshina Kannada/Udupi Muslim traders) and Navayath (Bhatkal's Arab-descended Muslim community) are distinct groups; do not use interchangeably.
- Sirsi's Marikamba Jatre, Udupi's Paryaya and Dharmasthala's administration are three separate institutions; do not merge their descriptions.
- The Sharavathi is a Malnad river, dammed and largely course-run through Shivamogga, that happens to meet the sea at Honnavar in Uttara Kannada — describe both halves accurately.
- Vasco da Gama's documented first Indian landfall (1498) was at Kappad, Kerala; St Mary's Islands (Malpe, Udupi) is only a locally cited tradition about his voyage, not an equivalent or competing fact.
- Treat the 2025 Dharmasthala mass-burial allegations and SIT investigation as contested and ongoing; never name any private individual in connection with it.
- Verify that the 2022 hijab controversy began at a Udupi government PU college, and check its 2026 pending-bench status.
- Do not present Uttara Kannada's ghat-top taluks (Sirsi, Siddapur, Yellapur, Haliyal, Mundgod, Joida, Dandeli) as demographically, economically or culturally identical to its coastal taluks; they are ecologically Malnad despite being administratively part of this region.
- High literacy/HDI and high sex ratio here are not evidence of an absence of educational or gender problems; both need explaining (private-school dominance; male out-migration), not a clean success story.
- Verify all serving officeholders and the sitting Chief Minister (D.K. Shivakumar since 3 June 2026) before naming anyone in a public role.

### 3.6 Sharp questions this dossier must answer
1. Why does a region with Karnataka's two highest district literacy rates (Dakshina Kannada, Udupi) still show a real secondary-stage dropout problem, unlike a poverty-driven north Karnataka pattern?
2. How does Gulf and Mumbai/Bengaluru out-migration change household decisions about keeping a teenager, especially a boy, in school through Class 10?
3. Why do Uttara Kannada's ghat-top taluks (Sirsi, Siddapur, Yellapur, Haliyal, Mundgod, Joida, Dandeli) show worse school-access and dropout indicators than the district's own coastal taluks?
4. What role does the region's unusually dense private/missionary-trust school network play in concentrating dropout risk among poorer, migrant-labour and Koraga/Siddi/Halakki-tribal households?
5. How do seasonal cashew- and fish-processing work and fishing-boat labour pull adolescents, especially girls, out of school in coastal Udupi and Uttara Kannada?
6. What do current, verified UDISE+ figures show at district level for all three districts, and how do they compare with the state secondary-dropout rate?
7. How did the January 2022 Udupi hijab dispute affect girls' continued enrolment and attendance since, and what is its verified current legal status?
8. How does the region's exceptionally high sex ratio, especially Udupi's, relate to schooling outcomes for the girls who remain, given it reflects male out-migration rather than gender-equal investment?
9. What do Bunt, Billava, Mogaveera, Koraga, Beary, Navayath, Mangalorean Catholic, Havyaka, Siddi and Halakki residents each say shapes a child's chances of finishing secondary school in their community?
10. How does the absence of a medical college/super-specialty hospital and of any airport in Uttara Kannada, against their concentration in Dakshina Kannada/Udupi, shape household schooling investment across the three districts?
11. What is the current, verified status of the Yettinahole project, the Kasturirangan/Western Ghats ESA dispute, the Seabird naval-base expansion and Kaiga's expansion, and how does each affect land, livelihoods and schooling?
12. How do residents describe the difference between "Tulu Nadu" and "Karavali," and what do Uttara Kannada residents say about being grouped with the more prosperous Tulu-speaking south?
13. What do local reporting and residents say actually happened at Dharmasthala in the 2025 mass-burial allegations, as distinct from what remains unproven or contested?
14. How do coastal Muslim (Beary/Navayath) households describe the "Gulf wife" pattern's effect on children's schooling and discipline?
