### PART A: core topics

**culture / Culture**
Definition: The customs, languages, religious practices, cuisine, arts, festivals, and social norms that characterize the way of life of a region's inhabitants.
Word target: 600
Must cover: staple foods and signature dishes (everyday, festive, ritual), drinks and food taboos; dress; the annual calendar of festivals, fairs, jatres and urus with months and places; folk and classical art forms with named troupes, artists or institutions; crafts and GI-tagged products; writers, poets and literary laureates; cinema and theatre; sports and traditional games; life-cycle and wedding customs; hospitality and etiquette norms; the heritage sites and symbols residents are proudest of. Language and religion have their own topics, so keep them to a pointer here.
Priority (P): the festival, fair and harvest calendar (when households travel, spend, marry or take children out of school); staple foods and food norms; three or four symbols of regional pride.

**education / Education**
Definition: The formal and informal systems of learning in a region, including schools, higher education, vocational training, literacy levels, and education policy implementation.
Word target: 900
Must cover: literacy by district (Census 2011, total and female, SC/ST) and later survey figures; the school network (government, aided, private; Kannada, English, Urdu, Marathi, Telugu, Tamil and other-medium schools; residential schools and hostels run by social-welfare, tribal, minority and backward-class departments; Kasturba Gandhi Balika Vidyalayas); enrolment, gross enrolment ratio and transition from primary to upper primary to secondary; DROPOUT AND OUT-OF-SCHOOL data from UDISE+ and from Karnataka's own SATS (Student Achievement Tracking System) records (by district, gender and social category, especially Classes 9-10) plus any state, NGO or academic study of why children leave school in this region; SSLC (Class 10) and PUC (Class 12) results and district rankings over the last five years and what local people say explains them; teacher availability (vacancies, guest teachers, pupil-teacher ratio, single-teacher schools); infrastructure (toilets, drinking water, electricity, libraries, digital access); distance to the nearest secondary school and how children get there (bicycle and bus-pass schemes); midday-meal (Akshara Dasoha), egg, milk, uniform and shoe schemes and how well they work here; learning outcomes (ASER, NAS); higher education (named universities, engineering, medical, first-grade and government colleges, ITIs, polytechnics) and where local students go; tuition and coaching culture; English-medium demand versus Kannada-medium tradition; the State Education Policy versus NEP debate; region-specific interventions and legal provisions. Child marriage and teenage pregnancy belong to women_children_youth: give them one clause here.
Priority (P): dropout and out-of-school figures by district, gender and social category at Classes 9-10 and the documented local reasons; distance and hostel access; teacher availability; SSLC/PUC pattern and explanations; language of instruction; scheme delivery.

**agriculture / Agriculture**
Definition: The cultivation of crops, animal husbandry, fisheries, and allied rural livelihood activities, including land use, irrigation, major produce, and farming communities.
Word target: 600
Must cover: principal crops with area and production by district; soil types and cropping calendar; irrigation (canals, tanks, borewells, rainfed share) and the projects that matter locally; horticulture and plantation crops; sericulture; animal husbandry and dairy (milk unions, Nandini/KMF); fisheries and aquaculture; forest produce; landholding structure (small and marginal share, tenancy, land-reform legacy); crop loss, crop insurance, loan waivers, farmer suicides, price crashes for named crops, MSP demands, input costs and labour shortage; farmers' unions and agrarian movements; markets (APMCs, cooperatives, processing units); mechanisation and natural farming; named local crop varieties and GI products. The physical events of drought and flood belong to environment_water_land: here cover their effect on crops, income and debt.
Priority (P): crops and area by district; rainfed versus irrigated share; price, debt and crop-loss distress and its timing; labour shortage; markets and input costs.

**health / Health**
Definition: The healthcare systems, public health conditions, disease burden, and health-related environmental risks affecting a region's population.
Word target: 600
Must cover: NFHS-5 (2019-21) district indicators (infant and under-five mortality, stunting, wasting, anaemia, institutional births, immunisation, sex ratio at birth, non-communicable disease, tobacco and alcohol); public facilities (district hospitals, medical colleges, CHC/PHC counts and vacancies) and the private sector; tertiary-care hubs and referral patterns; 108 ambulance and Arogya Karnataka/Ayushman Bharat coverage; diseases and health-linked environmental risks specific to this region (for example zoonoses, water-borne or vector-borne disease, fluorosis, arsenic, occupational disease, pesticide exposure, HIV); nutrition outcomes and programmes (Poshan); mental health; traditional and folk healing; distance and travel time to care; health effects of migration and of climate events. Child marriage and teenage pregnancy belong to women_children_youth; anganwadi functioning likewise.
Priority (P): NFHS-5 gaps against the state; facility and doctor vacancies; travel time and referral; region-specific disease and environmental risks; reliance on private care.

**economy / Economy**
Definition: The production, employment, income, and wealth structure of a region, including industry, services, agriculture, trade, and key economic institutions.
Word target: 600
Must cover: gross district domestic product and per-capita income (latest available, named edition) and how the region ranks in the state; sector shares; named industries, clusters, industrial areas and major employers by district; mining, IT/BT, textiles, agro-processing, tourism, ports, fisheries and trade as applicable; banking and cooperative institutions with local roots; remittances; MSMEs and the informal sector; unemployment and labour-force participation (PLFS, at the finest level actually published; if only state-level figures exist, give the state figure and label it as the state figure, never as a district figure); poverty (NITI Aayog Multidimensional Poverty Index); regional-imbalance debates (Nanjundappa Committee, Karnataka Human Development Report) and where each district stands; flagship projects, promised investments and stalled ones; cost of living. Mining and energy installations belong to environment_water_land; here cover only the jobs and revenue they generate.
Priority (P): sector base and district income rank; jobs and unemployment; key employers and clusters; regional-imbalance ranking.

**transport / Transport**
Definition: The mobility and logistics infrastructure of a region, including roads, railways, aviation, transit systems, and freight networks.
Word target: 600
Must cover: national and state highways and expressways that matter locally; railways (zones, key lines and stations, pending or stalled projects, electrification, flagship services); airports and airstrips (operating, under construction, promised); ports and jetties; the state road transport corporations serving the region and the impact of the Shakti free-travel scheme on ridership, women's mobility and school or college attendance; metro or suburban rail; ghat sections and road-closure problems; rural road connectivity (PMGSY); how children and women actually reach school, work and hospitals; ferries, bridges and last-mile gaps; freight movement (commodities and routes); congestion.
Priority (P): access to school, hospital and market; public-bus coverage and the Shakti effect; ghat, rail and air gaps; stalled projects.

**demographics / Demographics**
Definition: The size, distribution, composition, growth, and migration patterns of a region's population.
Word target: 600
Must cover: population by district (Census 2011, and projections labelled as projections), density, decadal growth, urban share, sex ratio and child sex ratio, age structure; SC, ST and OBC shares; religious composition (Census 2011); mother-tongue composition (Census 2011); household size; largest cities and towns; tribal groups with populations; slum populations; disability; fertility and ageing; in-migration and out-migration stock. This topic owns the Census counts for SC/ST, religion and mother tongue, and the migration stock; other topics use them by a clause without repeating the figure.
Priority (P): population, growth and urban share; sex ratio; SC/ST/religion/mother-tongue numbers; migration flows.

**governance / Governance**
Definition: The administrative, civic, and institutional structures through which a region is governed, including municipal bodies, policy frameworks, and inter-governmental mechanisms.
Word target: 600
Must cover: administrative divisions, districts, taluks, hoblis, gram panchayats, zilla and taluk panchayats, city corporations and urban local bodies, development authorities; regional development boards and special constitutional or statutory provisions that apply to the region and how they have delivered; the functioning of local bodies (devolution, finances, vacancies); bureaucratic and police structure; inter-state boundary disputes that involve this region; district rankings in national indices; Aspirational District programme status; corruption and Lokayukta cases of note; protest and grievance patterns; region-specific policy commissions and their reports. Election results, MLAs and MPs belong to politics_elections.
Priority (P): local-body functioning and finance; special boards and provisions and their delivery; district index rankings; grievance and protest patterns.

### PART B: depth topics

**history_identity / History and Regional Identity**
Definition: The historical formation of the region, its polities and administrative past, and how residents understand and assert their regional identity.
Word target: 450
Must cover: dynasties and polities that shaped the region; colonial or princely administrative affiliation (Mysore State, Madras or Bombay Presidency, Nizam's Hyderabad State, Coorg and so on) and its lingering effects on institutions, schooling, land tenure and language; the unification (Ekikarana) movement and 1 November 1956; renamings and what they mean to residents; freedom-movement episodes and local heroes; commemorations and memorial sites; the origins of regional-identity movements and separate-state, separate-language or special-status demands; historical grievances and sources of pride.
Priority (P): the historical affiliation that still shapes institutions; identity demands; pride and grievance narratives.

**language / Languages and Dialects**
Definition: The languages, dialects, scripts and speech habits of the region and the politics of language in schooling and public life.
Word target: 450
Must cover: the regional varieties of Kannada and their features; Tulu, Konkani, Kodava, Beary, Marathi, Telugu, Tamil, Urdu/Dakhni, Lambani and other languages present and where (mother-tongue counts belong to demographics); scripts; language of instruction and administration; language politics (Kannada-medium rules, Kannada Development Authority, linguistic-minority schools, Tulu official-language demand and similar); up to 10 illustrative local terms woven into the prose, in Roman transliteration with a gloss at first use, chosen for policy relevance (schooling, farming, land, water, household, governance), while the full glossary goes in Annex D; two or three typical proverbs or idioms with translation, each attributed to a source.
Priority (P): mismatch between language of instruction and home language; dialect distance from textbook Kannada; minority-language schooling; language politics.

**society_communities / Social Structure and Communities**
Definition: The caste, tribe, religious-community and social-hierarchy structure of the region, and the norms that govern family and marriage.
Word target: 600
Must cover: the major castes, sub-castes, tribes and religious communities present in each district, their approximate strength (state the source and year for every figure; Karnataka's caste data are contested, so cite the 2015 Karnataka Socio-Economic and Educational Survey and any later re-survey, stating its exact publication and official-acceptance status as of the as-of date, and never presenting an unpublished or leaked figure as settled fact), traditional occupations and current position; dominant communities and their organisations, mutts and associations; reservation categories that apply (SC/ST/Category 1, 2A, 2B, 3A, 3B, the internal-reservation classification of Scheduled Castes -- the Sadashiva Commission and Nagamohan Das Commission reports -- and the reservation percentages actually in force as of the as-of date, flagging any that are under litigation or stayed); kinship and inheritance systems (including matrilineal traditions where present); marriage customs; caste discrimination and atrocities as documented; inter-community relations and known flashpoints; migrant communities; community-run charitable and educational institutions. Gender norms and child marriage belong to women_children_youth.
Priority (P): community composition and dominance by district; reservation categories and debates; discrimination and flashpoints; community institutions that run schools and hostels.

**religion_belief / Religion and Belief**
Definition: Religious institutions, practices and pilgrimage systems, including folk belief, and their role in public and educational life.
Word target: 450
Must cover: dominant and minority faiths; mutts, temples, dargahs, churches, Jain and Buddhist centres, with names and locations; pilgrimage circuits and their economic weight; folk and spirit-worship traditions; syncretic shrines; the role of religious institutions in running schools, colleges, hospitals, hostels and free meals; religion in public controversy (this topic owns religious controversies: state the competing positions with sources and take no side); relevant laws and their local effects. Festival dates and places belong to culture.
Priority (P): institutions that run schools, hostels and meals; pilgrimage economies; public controversies with the competing positions stated.

**politics_elections / Political Landscape and Electoral Behaviour**
Definition: The party-political map of the region, voting patterns, leaders and the issues that mobilise voters.
Word target: 450
Must cover: 2023 Assembly and 2024 Lok Sabha results by district and constituency (seats by party) and the MLAs and MPs; historical voting patterns; the caste and community alignments behind them; leading political families and public leaders of the region (public figures only, in their public role); regional-development demands and how they are voiced; reservation agitations; the electoral role of farmers', Dalit, Kannada-pro and regional-identity organisations; local-body politics; the electoral reception of the five state guarantee schemes; the region's relationship with Bengaluru's political centre.
Priority (P): 2023 and 2024 seats by party; community alignments; the regional demands that mobilise voters; reception of the guarantees.

**environment_water_land / Environment, Water and Land**
Definition: The physical geography, climate, natural resources and hazards of the region and the disputes over water, forest and land.
Word target: 450
Must cover: physiography, altitude, rainfall and temperature, soils; rivers, dams, tanks and lakes; inter-state and intra-state water disputes and their local politics; groundwater depletion and quality; forests, national parks and wildlife, human-wildlife conflict; Western Ghats regulation debates where relevant; mining and quarrying; droughts, floods, landslides, heatwaves and urban flooding with years and places; pollution; energy installations (thermal, hydel, nuclear, solar, wind); displacement by dams and projects and the state of rehabilitation; land-tenure disputes and forest-rights issues.
Priority (P): drought, flood and landslide history by place and year; groundwater; water disputes; wildlife conflict; displacement.

**labour_migration / Labour, Migration and Livelihood Vulnerability**
Definition: Occupational structure, wages, seasonal and distress migration, child labour and the informal economy, and how these shape family life and children's schooling.
Word target: 450
Must cover: main occupations and wage rates (agricultural, construction, plantation, factory, gig); seasonal and distress out-migration (from where, to where, in which season, in which industry); in-migration of labour; child-labour hotspots and industries; bonded and contract labour; remittance economies (Gulf and others); left-behind children, seasonal hostels and schools for migrant children; unionisation; what happens to schooling in migration seasons. MGNREGA days and wages belong to welfare_public_services.
Priority (P): who migrates, where, when and why; wages; child labour; the effect on schooling.

**women_children_youth / Women, Children and Youth**
Definition: The condition, roles and aspirations of women, children, adolescents and young people in the region.
Word target: 450
Must cover: sex ratio, female literacy and female workforce participation; gender norms; child marriage and teenage pregnancy (NFHS-5, by district); harmful practices documented in the region (for example the devadasi/jogini system where applicable); anganwadi/ICDS functioning; self-help groups, microfinance and women's debt; violence against women and children (NCRB, POCSO figures); girls' schooling barriers (distance, safety, menstruation, sibling care, early marriage); children with disabilities; youth unemployment, aspirations, exam coaching and out-migration to cities; substance abuse; sports and youth organisations; the situation of transgender and other marginalised groups.
Priority (P): child marriage; girls' schooling barriers; women's work and self-help-group debt; youth jobs and aspirations; violence figures.

**welfare_public_services / Welfare and Public Services**
Definition: Delivery and reception of state and central welfare schemes and basic public services in the region.
Word target: 450
Must cover: the five state guarantees (Gruha Jyothi, Gruha Lakshmi, Anna Bhagya, Shakti, Yuva Nidhi) and how they are working locally; PDS/ration and food security; pensions and social security; MGNREGA days, wages and performance; housing; drinking water (Jal Jeevan Mission tap coverage by district) and sanitation; electricity supply for homes and pump-sets; scholarships and student welfare; land-records and digital services (Bhoomi, Seva Sindhu); grievance mechanisms; leakage and corruption complaints; the presence and role of NGOs, foundations and CSR programmes; how residents rate government versus private service delivery; for urban districts: piped-water supply and tanker dependence, solid-waste handling, housing and slum conditions, lakes, traffic and air quality, and the urban-local-body delivery gap. Shakti's transport impact belongs to transport and the guarantees' electoral reception to politics_elections.
Priority (P): local working of the five guarantees; PDS, pension, water and power delivery; scholarship delivery; grievance handling.

**worldview_voice / Regional Worldview and Voice**
Definition: How people of the region typically see themselves, their neighbours and the state, what they value and resent, how they frame problems, and where they get their information.
Word target: 600
Must cover: sources of regional pride; recurrent grievances (funds, water, jobs, connectivity, neglect by the state capital, cultural marginalisation); trust in institutions; attitudes to government versus private schooling, English versus Kannada, migration, women's work and education, farming as a career, and environment versus development; the information ecosystem (dominant Kannada and English newspapers and TV channels, local radio, WhatsApp and YouTube use, digital literacy, smartphone penetration); what outsiders get wrong about the region. PROBLEM FRAMING is the most important part of this topic (at least 40 percent of its words): for each of these seven problems write one or two sentences in the pattern "<Named survey, study or reporting, year> reports that residents attribute <problem> mainly to <A> and <B>, blame <C>, and ask for <D>": school dropout; drought and water stress; farm distress; healthcare access; unemployment and out-migration; girls' and women's schooling and safety; neglect by the state capital. If no evidence about this region exists for a problem, write no sentence for it and record the gap in Annex E. Never infer a view from statistics. Base every statement on a cited source and attribute it inside the sentence (for example "a 2018 CSDS-Lokniti survey found ... [S2]"). Use the labels widespread, contested or minority only where a survey or several independent sources support them; otherwise name who holds the view. Where a survey gives a share or plurality for a contested view, state it (for example "a majority ... while a substantial minority ..."); where no such data exists, use hedged framing ("many residents say ..., though others ...") rather than a flat list of positions, so this text cannot later be read as the region's single, uncontested opinion. Leave out humour, idiom and manner of speaking unless a documented source supports them. Write no stereotypes about a community, district or region, and describe how regions see each other only where a source documents it.
Priority (P): problem framing for the seven problems; recurrent grievances; trust in institutions.
